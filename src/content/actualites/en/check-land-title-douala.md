In Cameroon, the land title (“titre foncier”) is the official certificate of ownership of a piece of land. It is the document that proves a seller really owns what they are selling. Before you pay anything, take the time to check it. Here are the five checks we recommend to every buyer.

## 1. Ask for a copy of the land title

The seller should be able to show you the title and give you a copy. Note the title number, the owner’s name, the surface area and the location. A seller who refuses, or keeps putting it off, is a warning sign.

## 2. Get a recent certificate of ownership

This certificate (“certificat de propriété”) is issued by the land registry that covers the plot. It confirms who the current owner is and lists any charges registered against the title: a mortgage, a court caution or another restriction. Ask for a recent one, dated within the last few weeks. This is the single most important check.

## 3. Match the seller to the title

The name on the title must be that of the person selling, backed by an identity document. If the land belongs to several heirs or to a company, all the beneficiaries, or the legal representative, must sign.

## 4. Have the land checked on site

A sworn surveyor compares the plan attached to the title with what is on the ground: boundary markers, limits, surface area. They also check that the plot does not encroach on a road reserve, a drain or a no-build zone. While you are there, talk to the neighbours: they know the history of the land.

## 5. Sign before a notary

The sale of titled land goes through a notarial deed. The notary repeats the checks, drafts the deed and follows the transfer of the title into your name. Pay by bank transfer or cheque, against a receipt. Avoid cash handed to a go-between.

## Buying a plot on an estate

If you are buying a plot carved out of a larger piece of land, make sure the subdivision is under way and that you will receive an individual land title in your own name. A simple “sale certificate” or a signed plan is not a title.

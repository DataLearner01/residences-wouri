Douala keeps growing, and every neighbourhood has its own profile. There is no right area in absolute terms: there is the one that fits your plans. Living, letting and reselling do not lead to the same addresses. Here is how we see things for 2026.

## Bonapriso and Bonanjo: the safe choice

These are the most established central districts: government offices, company headquarters, international schools, restaurants. Land is scarce and prices are high. People buy a villa or an upmarket apartment here, to live in or to let to executives and expatriates. The entry cost is significant, but demand holds up.

## Akwa: the commercial heart

Akwa is where offices, banks and shops are concentrated. Studios and small apartments let easily to working people who want to live close to their jobs. It is a busy, dense district: check the parking, the water supply and the building’s generator.

## Bonamoussadi, Makepe, Kotto: the family choice

In the north of the city, these residential areas attract families: schools, markets, sports facilities, quieter streets. Two and three-bedroom apartments find takers, both to buy and to rent. Prices remain more affordable than in the centre. The point to watch: journey times at rush hour.

## Yassa and Japoma: a bet on the east

At the eastern gateway to Douala, the Yassa area has been changing since Japoma Stadium was built and the main roads were upgraded. You can still find good-sized plots at affordable prices. This is a medium-term choice: you buy today, build at your own pace, and the neighbourhood grows around you.

## Logbessou and the northern outskirts

Further out, new estates are multiplying. Prices are attractive, but quality varies widely from one project to the next. Here more than anywhere, check the land title, the state of the roads and whether water and electricity have really arrived.

## Three questions to ask yourself

1. **Who is it for?** You, your family, a tenant? The type of property follows from that.
2. **When do you need it?** A completed apartment earns from day one. A plot takes patience.
3. **With what guarantees?** A promising neighbourhood never makes up for a doubtful land title.
